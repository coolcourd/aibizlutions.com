What does a small business website need?

By AI Bizlutions. We build websites for small businesses, so keep that in mind. Published November 22, 2026.

A small business website needs to answer four questions fast: what you do, where you are, when you are open and how to reach you. That means a tap-to-call phone number, directions, your hours, your services or menu, real photos and reviews, all on a site that loads quickly and works well on a phone.

Help people reach you

  • Tap-to-call phone number. Near the top of every page, so someone on a phone can call you with one tap.
  • Address with directions. Your street address and a link that opens directions in their maps app.
  • Opening hours. Easy to find, kept up to date, and including holiday changes.
  • A simple contact form. Name, how to reach them, and their message. Short forms tend to get finished.
  • Booking or ordering, if you take them. A clear button that goes straight to it.

Show what you offer

  • A clear headline. One line that says what you do and where, such as "Family-run bakery in Mesa".
  • Your services or menu. Listed in full on the site as text, not only as a photo of a printed menu. Prices help where you can share them.
  • Your service area. If you go to customers, name the towns and neighborhoods you cover.
  • Real photos. Your place, your team and your work. People trust what looks real.

Earn trust

  • Reviews. A few real reviews from customers, with their first names, near the top.
  • An about section. Who you are, how long you have been doing this and why you care.
  • Your own web address. yourbusiness.com looks more established than a free address.
  • A secure site. The address starts with https and shows a lock in the browser.
  • A privacy page if your form collects personal details.

Work well everywhere

  • Phone-friendly. Text you can read without zooming, buttons big enough for a thumb, and nothing that slides sideways.
  • Fast. Pages that show up in a couple of seconds, even on a weak signal. Large photos are the usual reason a site is slow.
  • Easy to read. Short paragraphs, clear headings and dark text on a light background.
  • Easy to update. You, or whoever looks after the site, can change hours and prices in minutes.

Get found on Google

  • Matches your Google listing. The same business name, address, phone number and hours as your Google Business Profile. Mismatches confuse customers and search engines.
  • One page per main service. A page for each thing people search for can help you show up for more searches.
  • Page titles that say what and where. Each page has a clear title, like "Brake repair in Gilbert".
  • Linked from your listing and social pages. Put your web address on your Google listing, your social profiles and your printed materials.

What a small business website does not need

  • Pop-ups that cover the page the moment it opens
  • Music or video that plays on its own
  • Pages and pages of text nobody reads
  • A slideshow that hides your most important message

Common questions

How many pages does a small business website need?

Many local businesses do well with one strong home page plus a page for services or the menu, an about page and a contact page. A page for each main service can help you show up for more searches.

Do I need a blog?

No. A blog can help if you will keep it up, but an out-of-date blog looks worse than none. Clear service pages matter more.

Should my prices be on my website?

If you can share them, yes. Even a starting price or a range helps people decide to contact you. If every job is different, explain what affects the price.
