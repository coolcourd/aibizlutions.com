How much does a small business website cost?

By AI Bizlutions. We build websites for small businesses, so keep that in mind. Published November 22, 2026.

By our own rough estimates, a small business website usually costs anywhere from a few hundred dollars a year if you build it yourself, to about $1,000 to $5,000 with a freelancer, to $5,000 or more with an agency. On top of that, plan for a domain name (about $10 to $25 a year) and hosting and upkeep (about $5 to $100 a month). Ours is $699 plus $29 a month.

Do-it-yourself website builders

Do-it-yourself website builders let you pick a template and drag your words and photos into place. The plan is usually about $15 to $50 a month. Plans with online payments or a shop often cost more.

The real cost is your time. A first site often takes a few weekends, or longer if you are writing everything from scratch, choosing photos and learning the tool. If you enjoy that kind of work and have the time, this is the cheapest route.

  • Good for: very small budgets, people who like doing it themselves
  • Watch for: the monthly fee rising after an intro price, and how hard it is to move your site later

Freelancers

A freelance web designer usually charges about $1,000 to $5,000 for a small business site. Some charge by the hour, often somewhere around $50 to $150 an hour. You get a person who builds it for you and can shape it around your business.

Quality varies, so ask to see sites they have built for businesses like yours, and ask who hosts the site and who you call when something breaks a year from now.

  • Good for: a custom look without agency prices
  • Ask about: ongoing costs, and how you would make changes later

Agencies

A web agency is a team: designers, writers and developers. A small business site from an agency often starts around $5,000 and can run to $25,000 or more. Many agencies also offer monthly plans for updates, help showing up on Google, and reports.

That price usually buys planning, original writing, custom design and more polish. For a business with complex needs or a big marketing budget, it can be worth it. For a local shop that needs a clear, good-looking site, it is often more than you need.

The costs that keep coming every year

Whichever route you choose, a website has running costs. Plan for these:

  • Domain name: your web address, usually about $10 to $25 a year.
  • Hosting: the computer that keeps your site online, usually about $5 to $50 a month on its own, or included in a builder plan or a monthly upkeep plan.
  • Updates: new hours, prices, photos and fixes. Either your own time, a monthly plan where someone keeps the site updated, or paying someone by the hour.
  • Business email (optional): an address like you@yourbusiness.com, often a few dollars a month per person.
  • Extras: online booking, ordering or payment tools can add their own monthly or per-sale fees.

How to keep the cost down

  • Start with the pages people actually use: what you do, where you are, your hours and how to reach you.
  • Use your own photos. They cost nothing and look more honest than stock pictures.
  • Have your words ready before work starts. Waiting on content is one of the most common reasons projects run long.
  • Ask for every ongoing cost in writing before you agree to anything.

Common questions

Is a cheap website worth it?

It can be, if it is clear, loads fast and works well on a phone. A simple site that answers what, where, when and how to contact you is worth more than an expensive one that is hard to use.

Do I have to pay every month?

Almost always, yes. Every site needs a domain name and hosting to stay online. The question is whether you pay for them separately, inside a builder plan or inside a monthly upkeep plan.

Who should own my domain name?

You should. Make sure the domain is registered in your name or your business's name, so you can move your site later if you ever want to.
