Do I need a website if I have a Google listing or Facebook page?

By AI Bizlutions. Published November 22, 2026.

Keep your Google listing and Facebook page, they are essential and often the first place people find you. But most established local businesses still benefit from a website, because it is the one page you own and control. It holds your full services or menu, builds trust, helps you show up for more searches and does not depend on anyone's feed.

What your Google listing does well

Your Google Business Profile is often the first thing people see when they search for you or for what you sell nearby. It shows your hours, phone number, directions, photos and reviews in one place. If you have not claimed yours, do that first. It is free and it matters.

What your Facebook page does well

A Facebook page or other social profile is great for news, photos, events and talking with regulars. It shows people your business is active and lets them message you.

What a website adds that they cannot

A page you own

Your listing and your social pages live on someone else's platform, under their rules. They can change the layout, limit what you can show or hide your posts. Your website is yours. Think of the listing as a sign on a busy road and your website as your own shop.

Room for everything you offer

A listing has limited space. A website can show your full menu or every service, prices, your service area, how a job works and answers to common questions, in your own words.

More trust

Many customers look for a website before they call, book or drive over. A clear, current site tells them you are a real, established business that cares about the details.

Showing up for more searches

Your listing mostly shows up when people search for your name or your kind of business on a map. A website with a page for each service can also show up when people search for the specific thing you do, like "kitchen tile repair" or "gluten-free birthday cake".

No feed deciding who sees you

On social media, a feed decides how many people see each post. Your website is there for anyone who looks, any time, and it does not drop out of sight after a day.

When you might not need a website yet

If your business is brand new, you are still testing the idea, or you are not on Google yet, a website can wait. Start by building your brand, claiming your Google listing and getting the word out. Come back to a website once customers are looking for you.

The best setup for most local businesses

  • A claimed, complete Google listing with your website address on it
  • An active social page for news and photos
  • A website with the same name, address, phone number and hours, that holds everything else

Each one points to the others. Together they make you easier to find and easier to trust.

Common questions

Can my Facebook page be my website?

It can stand in for a while, but it is not the same. Some people do not use Facebook, the page follows Facebook's rules, and it is harder to show your full services or menu in a way that is easy to find.

Does having a website help my Google listing?

A website that matches your listing gives Google, and customers, more information about your business. It does not replace the listing, and the listing does not replace it. They work best together.

Is a one-page website enough?

For many local businesses, yes, to start. A single clear page with your services, hours, location, photos, reviews and contact details covers what most visitors need.
